      How EZDES plans a shift

      Workflow

      1. Load a workbook (or the newest file from the shared folder when running on the server).
      2. Check the detected columns. Only Copperweight is required; WO, TN and cores improve the plan.
      3. Pick the day, shift and either “Fill the shift” or a fixed number of jobs. The schedule rebuilds as you go.
      4. Adjust order, pin jobs, add notes, then print or export.

      Jobs

      WO 174-05-01 and 174-05-08 are one job (174-05). A job is scheduled whole so it finishes the same day. With “Next shift continues the line” ticked, the last job may run into the next shift's list; it is marked “Next shift”, and the planner prefers one at the speed the shift ends on. Lines without a WO use TN, then their own row.

      The workbook lists whole orders even when part of one has run. Tick Done on cores that are finished: they drop out of the plan, are listed under it, and stay ticked when the same core appears in later workbooks.

      Ordering

      • Which jobs make the shift is decided by score. Each weight gets a day score: by default Monday favours light weights, Wednesday is balanced and Friday favours heavy ones. Edit them under “Day scores”. Due dates and waiting time add to it.
      • Waiting: every production day since a job first appeared in a loaded workbook adds +10, up to +100. A light job squeezed out on Thursday therefore climbs until it gets in. The first-seen dates are kept in this browser.
      • The order inside the shift is the fastest one. Every order of the weight blocks is tried; usually that means fast → slow (h → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4), because speeding up means waiting for the line to clear. Jobs due today or overdue are the exception: each hour they would wait costs their urgency points in minutes, so they move to the front unless that costs a lot more time. Lines run one weight/speed block at a time; inside a block each job's lines stay together, most urgent job first.
      • The summary shows how many minutes that order saves compared with running in day-score order.
      • A job with lines in two weights runs last in the first block and first in the next, so it doesn't stop and set up again.
      • “On the machine now” is the weight running when the shift starts. By default (Auto) it's where the latest earlier plan in this browser ended; a note above the plan says which. Pick a weight or “Nothing” if the machine isn't where the plan left it. It goes back to Auto when you change the day or date.
      • With “Fill the shift”, jobs are added in score order while they fit, then any time the ordering saved is offered to the jobs left out.
      • Pinned jobs always go first and are always included. Manual moves are kept until you reset the order.
      • A job with a line that has no core-weight rule is left out (and listed in Data checks) instead of being timed as 0 minutes.

      Due dates

      • Map a Due date column to use them. A job counts as due on its earliest line's date, compared with the production date.
      • Urgency counts production days (Mon–Fri): planning Friday, a job due Monday is due the next work day, and one due Saturday is due today.
      • Long jobs start earlier. A job that needs 2 shifts and is due the next work day scores as due today.
      • Urgency points: overdue +100, due that day +80, next work day +50, within 3 work days +25, within 7 +10. Overdue always beats the day's weight preference. “Strong priority” doubles the points; “Ignore” turns them off.
      • Urgent jobs are picked first. Jobs due today or overdue also run early in the shift; weight blocks are never split for them.
      • Dates like 2026-11-16, 11/16/2026 and 16 Nov 2026 are understood. Slashed dates are read month-first unless the first number is over 12.
      • If a job due today or earlier doesn't fit the shift, the summary says so.

      Timing

      • The clock is the loader's. The conveyor sets the gap, so a line of n cores takes n × “next core” seconds to feed. “Load” is when its first core goes on; “Out” is when its last core comes out, one travel time later.
      • At a speed change the next weight can't be fed until the last core is far enough through: fully out when slowing down (a light core stuck at a slow speed is ruined), out of the etcher when speeding up. Those waits are the two “last core at (% of line)” settings, plus the minutes to change the setting itself: one default, or a per-pair number in the “Changing the speed setting” table under Core-weight rules. The order search counts them, so it finds the quickest direction on its own.
      • Setup minutes are added whenever a new job starts (0 if a WO/TN change costs nothing).
      • A job that runs a little off its weight's usual speed (the sheet's Speed column) is its own speed block, so the line clears before and after it even if the speeds are only 2 apart.
      • If you measure the line, enter its length and the core pitch under Core-weight rules: travel and interval are then computed from each speed instead of typed.
      • Mixed cores (e.g. 1/2) run at the heavier side's speed. Mark one “Bottom image” when the lighter side isn't clear: setting the diverters costs “Bottom image setup” minutes when a run of image cores starts. Image cores are kept together, and carried across a weight change when possible, so this is paid as few times as possible.
      • Breaks come off the shift length.
